## What is a business growth strategy?

A business growth strategy is a short, written answer to three questions: **who you serve, why they should choose you, and how they will find you.** Everything else (your website, your social media, your ads, your SEO) should follow from those answers.

Most small businesses skip this step. They post on Facebook because everyone does, run ads because someone suggested it, and redesign the website every few years, without a clear thread connecting any of it. The result is effort that feels busy but doesn't move the numbers.

A good growth plan fixes that by deciding **what not to do** as much as what to do.

## Why most growth plans fail

In my experience, plans rarely fail because the ideas are bad. They fail because:

- **There are too many priorities.** A plan with 30 actions becomes a plan with none.
- **Nobody owns the work.** If every task belongs to "the team", it belongs to no one.
- **Nothing is measured.** Without two or three clear numbers, you can't tell what's working.
- **It's written for a bigger company.** Advice built for a 200-person team doesn't fit a business with five people.

So every plan I write is short, prioritised, has a name next to every task, and fits the team you actually have.

## What's in your 90-day growth plan

| Part | What it answers |
|---|---|
| **Positioning** | Who exactly you serve, the problem you solve, and why you rather than a competitor |
| **Offer** | What you sell, how it's priced and packaged, and what makes it easy to say yes |
| **Channels** | Where your customers already look: Google, Maps, social media, referrals, partners |
| **Website & follow-up** | Whether your website and enquiry process turn interest into customers |
| **Priorities** | The three to five actions for the next 90 days, in order, with owners |
| **Numbers** | The few metrics that tell you it's working: enquiries, bookings, sales, cost per customer |

## Growth planning for businesses in Nepal and abroad

I'm based in Pokhara and work with businesses across Nepal, as well as clients abroad. Local businesses often have different realities from international ones: customers who decide on WhatsApp and Facebook, word of mouth that matters more than ads, and Google Maps doing more work than the website. A useful plan takes those realities into account rather than copying a template from somewhere else.
